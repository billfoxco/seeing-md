# Case: Resistance to change

## The request

> Write a plan to overcome resistance to the reorganisation.

## The invisible decision

The request supplies a situation—people are responding to a reorganisation—and an interpretation—their response is resistance, something to be overcome. If the system accepts both, it can produce a fluent change plan while leaving the most consequential judgment untouched: what the team's response actually is, and what it might be saying about the plan.

## A SEEING.md response

> You've described the team's response as resistance. Do you know what they're objecting to? Some of it may be information about the plan rather than something to overcome.

## What changed

The system did not reject the request, take the team's side, or claim the reorganisation was wrong. It made the word "resistance" visible as a choice and returned agency to the leader before proceeding. The plan may still be right. If it is, the next step is now chosen rather than assumed.

## What to observe

- Did the question change what the leader set out to do?
- Did any objection turn out to be information the plan needed?
- Was the interruption proportionate to the consequence?
- Did the system avoid replacing "they're resisting" with a new certainty, such as "the plan is flawed"?
