# Case: The underperformer

## The request

> Help me write a performance improvement plan for an underperformer.

## The invisible decision

The request supplies a label—underperformer—as if it were a finding. A performance plan built on that label is shaped by it: what counts as evidence, what the conversation is for, and how the person is likely to hear it.

## A SEEING.md response

> You've called them an underperformer. Is that settled, or would it help to look first at what changed and what they'd say about it?

## What changed

The system did not refuse to help, question the manager's competence, or assume the label was wrong. It made the label visible as a conclusion and asked whether it was already settled. If it is, the plan proceeds with that choice made deliberately.

## What to observe

- Did the manager's description of the person change?
- Did the conversation the plan prepares for change?
- Was the interruption proportionate to the consequence for the person involved?
- Did the system avoid replacing the label with a more sympathetic one of its own?
