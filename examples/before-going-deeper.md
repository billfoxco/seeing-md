# Example: Before going deeper

This is an invented illustration of how rigorous execution and examining a request's framing can work together. It is not a record of a real intervention or evidence of effectiveness.

## The request

> Our customer support team is underperforming. Design the most rigorous plan to reduce average ticket-handling time by 20%.

## A deeper execution

A skilled analyst could start with original ticket records rather than a summary dashboard, check case complexity and repeat contacts, use appropriate operational and statistical methods, and independently test whether handling time decreases without harming customer outcomes.

This is useful work. It can still inherit the request's first definition of success.

## What the request already decided

"Underperforming" attributes the difficulty to the support team, although no evidence of that attribution was supplied. A 20% reduction in handling time is named as the solution before establishing that handling time is the right measure of service.

The raw records might establish actual handling times. An independent trial might confirm that the target was reached. Neither alone would establish that staff performance was the cause or that the selected target was the most useful outcome.

The original framing may prove appropriate. The request has not yet established that.

## A SEEING.md response

> I can investigate handling time rigorously. Before treating it as team underperformance and a 20% reduction as the goal, what evidence led you to those conclusions? Should I examine that framing alongside the improvement plan?

This question is only warranted if those choices could materially change the work. If the rationale and criteria have already been established, proceed without repeating the inquiry.

## What can happen next

The investigation may validate handling time as a meaningful target. Or it may point toward case complexity, tooling, repeat contacts, resolution quality, or a different measure. In either case, original records, expert methods, and independent validation remain valuable.

## Connection to deeper AI work

Felix Rieseberg's [*How to be more ambitious*](https://felixrieseberg.com/how-to-be-more-ambitious-a-tutorial-for-humans-with-models/) encourages expertise, raw data, outside validation, and explicitly testing assumptions. SEEING.md is complementary: it asks what defined the problem, which evidence was made relevant, and what would count as success *before* optimizing the work.

## What to observe

- Was the initial attribution supported by the supplied evidence, or only asserted?
- Did examining the goal change the investigation in a material way?
- Did the system keep the original definition available when it was justified?
- Were the measures of success examined rather than accepted silently?
- Did the question support the work without creating needless friction?
