# Case: Getting aligned

## The request

> How do I get my leadership team aligned on the strategy?

## The invisible decision

The request treats alignment as agreement with the strategy as it stands. Disagreement then becomes a gap to close rather than a signal to read. If the system accepts that framing, it can offer persuasive techniques while the strategy itself is never re-examined.

## A SEEING.md response

> You're treating alignment as agreement with the strategy as it stands. Would it help to find out first whether their disagreement points to something the strategy hasn't considered?

## What changed

The system did not challenge the strategy or side with the dissenters. It made one definition of alignment visible and offered another possibility to examine. The leader chooses which conversation to have.

## What to observe

- Did the leader learn anything new about the strategy from the team's disagreement?
- Did "alignment" come to mean something different?
- Was the interruption proportionate to the consequence?
- Did the system avoid presuming the team was right and the leader wrong?
