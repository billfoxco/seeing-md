# Example: Reviewing a workspace snapshot

This is an illustrative case using invented content, not a transcript of a user's review or evidence of effectiveness.

## The supplied material

A person selects two projects for review:

- **Book:** Status: “Draft ready for another reading.” Next action: empty.
- **Website:** Status: “Opening page revised.” Next action: “Read it as a first-time visitor.”

No activity history or explanation for the empty field is supplied.

## The interpretive risk

A summary could call the book stalled, infer avoidance, or claim that the website is progressing faster. The snapshot does not establish any of those conclusions. It records two statuses and one next action.

## A possible response

The book is recorded as ready for another reading; no next action is recorded. The website's next action is to read the revised opening as a first-time visitor.

Both entries concern reading work again. That may be a useful connection to consider, but the notes do not establish a shared obstacle, priority, or change over time.

## What to observe

- Does the review distinguish the supplied notes from its interpretation?
- Can the reader trace each observation to a selected item?
- Does it preserve missing information without turning it into a judgment?
- Is the proposed connection useful, or does it merely make the review sound insightful?
- Can the person disagree without the system treating disagreement as confirmation?

No follow-up question is required merely to complete the review. An adequate description may be enough.
